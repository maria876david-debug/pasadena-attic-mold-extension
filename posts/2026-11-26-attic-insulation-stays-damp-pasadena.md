# Attic Insulation That Stays Damp: Why Pasadena Roofs Trap Moisture in Winter

Pasadena winters are mild, but they still bring rain — and rain is when attics show their weakness. Insulation that stays damp through the winter is one of the most common attic problems in the area, and it is easy to miss because nobody goes up to look while it is raining.

## How the moisture gets in

Damp insulation has three usual sources. The first is a small roof leak — a cracked vent boot, a lifted shingle, a flashing joint that has opened up — letting rain in a little at a time. The second is condensation: warm, moist air from the house rises into a cold attic and condenses on the underside of the roof deck, then drips into the insulation below. The third is the most avoidable of all: a bathroom fan that vents into the attic instead of outside, pumping steam into the insulation every single shower.

## Why damp insulation is a bigger problem than it looks

Wet insulation does not just get damp — it stops working. Insulation keeps heat in because of the air trapped in its fibres, and water replaces that air. Damp insulation insulates poorly, so heating bills rise and rooms feel colder. Worse, it becomes food for mould: organic dust, warmth from the house below, and steady moisture is everything mould needs. And once insulation is soaked through, it does not dry out on its own — it stays damp, compacted and useless until it is removed.

## The signs you can check without climbing in

You do not need to crawl through the attic to spot the problem. A musty smell in the upstairs rooms after rain, a ceiling stain that appears in winter and fades in summer, or insulation that looks dark, flattened or matted when you peek through the hatch — all of these point to moisture. If the attic smells damp after the first winter rain, go and look while the problem is still small.

## What fixes it properly

The fix has two parts, and both matter. First, stop the water: repair the leak, vent the bathroom fan outside the roof, and make sure the attic has airflow so moist air can escape instead of condensing. Second, remove insulation that is soaked through — drying it in place does not restore it. A dry attic with proper ventilation and dry insulation stays dry all winter; a patched leak with wet insulation left in place is a mould problem waiting for spring.
