# Wet Insulation After a Roof Leak: Why It Has to Dry or Be Replaced

After a roof leak, the shingles get the attention - but the insulation took the water. Wet insulation fails twice: it stops insulating, because waterlogged fibres cannot trap air, and it becomes a food-and-moisture bed for mould sitting right against your ceiling and joists. Whether it can be saved depends on the type and how long it stayed wet. Fibreglass batts that got briefly damp can sometimes dry in place with strong ventilation; blown-in cellulose holds water like a sponge and usually has to come out. The test is simple and honest: if it is still damp days after the leak stopped, or it smells musty, it is not drying - it is growing something. Check the attic a week after any leak repair, not just on the day. Dry insulation is invisible and does its job for decades; wet insulation quietly undoes both.
