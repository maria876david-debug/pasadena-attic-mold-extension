# How to Tell If Attic Mould Is Active or Old in a Pasadena Home

Finding mould in your Pasadena attic raises an immediate question: is this growing right now, or is it an old stain from a leak that was fixed years ago? The answer decides whether you need urgent work or just monitoring — and getting it wrong in either direction costs money.

## The moisture test

Mould needs moisture to grow. If the wood around the stain is dry to the touch and a moisture meter reads the same as the dry parts of the attic, the mould is probably dormant — left over from an old leak. If the wood is damp, or the stain sits under a roof valley, a vent, or a spot where winter rain gets in, the mould is likely still active. Damp wood plus mould growth is a current problem, not a historical one.

## What active mould looks like

Active mould growth tends to look fuzzy or powdery at the edges, and the affected area often grows between inspections. It frequently comes with a musty smell that gets stronger on damp days. Old, dormant mould looks flat and dry, with hard edges, and it doesn't change from one season to the next. Smell is a useful clue: a strong musty odour in the attic, especially after rain, usually means moisture is still getting in somewhere.

## The leak hunt comes first

Before any cleaning, find out where the moisture came from. In Pasadena attics, the usual suspects are roof leaks around flashing and valleys, bathroom vents that dump moist air into the attic instead of outside, and condensation where warm house air meets a cold roof deck in winter. Cleaning mould without fixing the moisture source is a short-term cosmetic job — the growth comes back as soon as conditions allow.

## Why "just bleach it" doesn't work

Bleach on a hard surface can kill surface mould, but attic mould grows into porous wood. Surface cleaning leaves the roots in the wood, and it does nothing about the moisture that let it grow. Proper remediation removes the affected material where needed, treats what remains, and — most importantly — fixes the moisture source so the problem doesn't return.

## When to call a professional

Call one if the affected area is larger than a few square feet, if anyone in the household has breathing problems or allergies, if you can't find the moisture source, or if the mould keeps coming back after cleaning. A professional inspection checks the roof, the vents, the insulation and the moisture readings together — because attic mould is usually a symptom, and the cause is somewhere else in the house.
